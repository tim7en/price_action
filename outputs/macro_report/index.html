Regime Taxonomy

The Labels This Macro App Watches

Regime label

Disinflationary Growth

Growth is healthy enough to support risk assets while inflation pressure is easing or contained.

Playbook: Trend-following and normal risk budgets can work when credit and volatility stay calm.

Failure mode: The failure mode is complacency: rich valuation plus a fresh VIX or rate shock.

Regime label

Liquidity Bubble Or Valuation Stretch

Valuation and momentum are running ahead of the macro margin of safety while volatility and credit still look benign.

Playbook: Do not short richness on its own, but react faster to failed breakouts, VIX turns, and front-end repricing.

Failure mode: Once liquidity conditions change, valuation can turn from tailwind to vulnerability very quickly.

Regime label

Inflationary Boom

Nominal growth and inflation are both strong, which tends to help real assets and cash-flow-now businesses more than long duration.

Playbook: Favor price action confirmed by real activity, commodities, and sturdy balance sheets.

Failure mode: If production weakens while inflation stays elevated, the boom usually degrades into stagflation.

Regime label

Stagflation Squeeze

Inflation stays stubborn while production and labor momentum soften, squeezing both margins and multiples.

Playbook: Reduce broad long exposure, demand stronger confirmation, and lean toward defensive or inflation-linked exposures.

Failure mode: Policy has little room to rescue markets without worsening inflation credibility.

Regime label

Rate-Shock Regime

Front-end or real-yield repricing is moving fast enough to hit long-duration assets before credit fully breaks.

Playbook: Penalize duration sensitivity, reduce leverage, and watch rate-shock interactions with expensive valuation.

Failure mode: A rate shock often becomes a credit shock with a lag.

Regime label

Credit Deleveraging

Credit spreads and financial conditions are tightening enough that the market is starting to transmit stress into the real economy.

Playbook: Trade less, lower risk budgets, and wait for credit stabilization before re-risking.

Failure mode: Cross-asset diversification weakens when refinancing stress turns broad.

Regime label

Panic Or Forced Liquidation

Immediate stress is overwhelming the system: VIX spikes, credit widens, and market liquidity thins out.

Playbook: Survival matters more than signal frequency. Cut leverage and treat price moves in volatility-adjusted terms.

Failure mode: Normal model assumptions break when price movement becomes discontinuous.

Regime label

Recovery And Reflation

Stress is still elevated, but the direction of stress is improving faster than the official macro data.

Playbook: Allow recovery signals when VIX and credit improve together, even if growth data still looks ugly.

Failure mode: Bear-market rallies fail when credit does not confirm the improvement.

Regime label

Sideways Low-Volatility Regime

Growth and inflation are stable enough that carry and mean reversion dominate while breakouts lose urgency.

Playbook: Keep breakout confidence modest and watch for the first low-to-rising volatility transition.

Failure mode: The first volatility expansion can invalidate months of low-vol assumptions.

Regime label

Fragile Late-Cycle Watch

Valuation is rich, volatility is elevated, and growth is no longer broad, but credit has not fully cracked yet.

Playbook: Treat the market as conditionally risk-on: participate selectively, but let credit and volatility control exposure quality.

Failure mode: A spread blowout, oil shock, or new rate repricing can harden this into a much more hostile regime.

System Design

A Better Macro Architecture

Regime Layer

This layer decides whether the environment is calm, stressed, panicking, recovering, or inflation-shocked before any directional forecast is trusted.

  • VIX level, change, percentile, and term structure
  • Credit and liquidity stress via high-yield spreads, NFCI, and dollar strength
  • Rate shock via front-end repricing and curve deformation
  • Inflation breadth and production breadth so sticky inflation is not confused with a temporary commodity shock

Prediction Layer

Once regime is known, price-action and macro features forecast forward return within that environment instead of pretending all regimes behave the same way.

  • Price action and volume
  • Macro, inflation, production, and valuation context
  • Volatility-adjusted returns and breakout strength
  • Sentiment and liquidity inputs when available

Decision Layer

The trading decision should use regime and return forecasts to decide not only direction but exposure quality.

  • Trade or no trade
  • Position size
  • Stop distance
  • Leverage and cooldown
  • Which strategy family is active

Interpretation Guardrails

How These Variables Should Be Read

VIX gets a structural role, not a manual weight

VIX is option-implied 30-day stress, not a generic input beside RSI. It should affect participation, sizing, stops, leverage, and strategy selection, not only the probability score.

Normalize price moves by implied volatility

A 2% move at low VIX is abnormal; the same move at high VIX may be ordinary. Returns, breakouts, drawdowns, and volume spikes become more informative when scaled by implied volatility.

Valuation is a vulnerability multiplier

High CAPE or market-cap-to-GDP does not mean crash tomorrow. It lowers long-run expected return and becomes dangerous when combined with rising volatility, widening spreads, or rapid tightening.

Decompose inflation before declaring a regime

Headline CPI can fall because energy rolled over while shelter and core stay sticky. The regime should react to the breadth of inflation, not just the headline print.

Rate pace matters more than static levels

Equities usually react hardest to speed, surprise, and direction of rate change. Front-end repricing, real-yield moves, and curve change deserve their own shock variables.

Production confirms or rejects the market narrative

Industrial and manufacturing output tell you whether real activity is improving underneath price action. If production is rolling over while risk assets levitate, fragility is rising.

Interactions matter more than isolated readings

High valuation alone is not enough, and high VIX alone is not enough. The dangerous state is the interaction: high valuation plus rising VIX plus fast rate hikes plus negative momentum plus credit stress.

Scenario Playbook

How The Macro Stack Should Influence Trading Decisions

High valuation, low VIX

The market is expensive but not yet stressed. Lower long-run expected return, but do not force a short solely because valuation is rich.

High valuation, rising VIX

Fragility is starting to activate. Reduce long exposure, tighten risk, and demand stronger confirmation before trusting breakouts.

High valuation, rising VIX, fast rate hikes

This is closer to a crash-risk regime. Avoid leveraged longs, prefer defense, and treat liquidity-sensitive assets as vulnerable.

High VIX but falling

Stress is still elevated, but the direction of stress is improving. This can be a recovery or relief-rally regime rather than a pure panic regime.

Low VIX, strong trend

Trend following can work well, but complacency risk rises if valuation is stretched and the front end starts repricing higher.

Sticky core and shelter inflation, weakening production

This is the stagflation squeeze. Growth assets lose support, duration can struggle, and risk control matters more than chasing nominal rebounds.

Energy spike, stable core, resilient production

Treat this as a commodity shock first, not an automatic persistent inflation regime. Watch whether the shock bleeds into core and labor before overreacting.
