Walk-Forward Regime Timing Test

Can the Dalio-style GMM macro regimes time the market when fit causally (refit every month on an expanding window, clusters characterised only by returns known at the time)? Evaluated on the Wilshire total-market index — no survivorship bias — over 2009-03 → 2026-04 (204 monthly refits), against the market-health ladder from the sector trend study, with identical carry costs.

Verdict

The walk-forward Dalio-regime signal does not improve on buy-&-hold once fit causally — its full-sample elegance does not survive real-time use. Keep the GMM as narrative context, not as a sizing input.

Method & caveats

  • Pre-registered mapping. Exposure is linear in the cluster's past-performance rank, from −1× (worst) to +3× (best): no tunable thresholds. k=5, seed 7, first fit after 120 months.
  • Label churn is real. Refitting monthly re-draws cluster boundaries; the exposure path (not the label names) is the tradable object, and its turnover is charged.
  • The health ladder's thresholds were tuned in-sample in the original study, so it carries a small hindsight advantage in this race; the GMM mapping does not.
  • One asset, ~16 years, ~2 full cycles. Statistical power is limited; treat the verdict as evidence, not proof.
