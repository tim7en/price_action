Method

The report reuses the same monthly macro regime engine as the macro atlas, then scores sector ETF proxies by 1-year and 3-year forward returns, future drawdown, and higher-high frequency inside matching regimes. No point-in-time analyst EPS feed exists in this repo. The sector entry calls below therefore use earnings-sensitivity proxies plus realized 1-year and 3-year forward returns from historically similar macro regimes.

Holdout Benchmark

ML Rotation, Reserve Cash Rule, And SPY

The benchmark uses only final holdout dates. Signals are generated from walk-forward out-of-sample sector predictions, executed one bar after the signal date, and held for five bars before the next rebalance. The quality-weighted variant mixes live ensemble probability with a sector quality prior estimated only from the pre-holdout validation window. The reserve-cash rule keeps 40% of the portfolio in cash earning 5% annually, deploys 10% of that reserve after a 5% SPY drawdown, another 10% after a 10% drawdown, and the rest after a 20% drawdown, then rebuilds the reserve only after SPY gets back to its prior high. The leveraged scenario applies 3x gross exposure and charges 6% annual interest on the borrowed 2x capital over each realized holding period.

Sector Map

The Equity Buckets Scored In This Report

XLE · Real asset cyclical

Energy

High earnings leverage to oil, capex, and nominal GDP.

Often benefits most when inflation and supply shocks dominate the tape.

XLB · Cyclical inflation beta

Commodity / Materials

Sensitive to industrial demand, pricing power, and commodity volumes.

Usually improves when nominal growth and input scarcity rise together.

XLI · Growth and capex cyclical

Manufacturing / Industrials

Leverages industrial production, capex, and freight intensity.

Tends to lead in recovery and reflation regimes when production broadens.

XLY · Consumer cyclical

Retail / Consumer Discretionary

Most exposed to real income, labor resilience, and disinflation relief.

Usually wants falling input pressure, healthy labor, and calm credit.

XLK · Duration and innovation

Information Technology

Benefits from disinflation, lower discount rates, and enterprise capex strength.

Typically strongest in disinflationary growth or liquidity-led melt-up regimes.

XLP · Defensive cash flow

Consumer Staples

Lower-volatility earnings with less macro beta than cyclical retail.

Acts as a defensive buffer when growth is fading or rate stress rises.

XLV · Defensive quality

Health Care

Relatively stable earnings stream with lower sensitivity to the goods cycle.

Useful when the portfolio needs defense without fully abandoning equity exposure.

XLU · Rate-sensitive defense

Utilities

Stable regulated cash flows, but still sensitive to rate and duration pressure.

Can cushion drawdowns when stress rises, though not every inflation regime helps it.
